Use case: logo-brand. Draw one finished square app-icon concept for BeeCount, a personal expense journal. The friendly visual joke: a plump little honeybee has dived headfirst into one enormous flower; only its striped bottom and wings are sticking out. This should have the charm of an independent illustrator's stamp, reduced to a distinctive flat symbol for a phone. Three-quarter rear view, diagonal arrangement: the blossom toward the lower LEFT, the bee's striped upturned rump toward the upper RIGHT. One bold warm-ivory five-petal flower seen from a slight angle; use five uneven, broad, chunky petals and just enough overlap to read as a daisy. Hide the flower's center behind the head of the bee, so there is NO visible face: the disappearing head and the jaunty upturned rump tell the whole story. A fat golden oval abdomen with two bold slightly curved graphite bands, a tiny blunt dark tip, and two little asymmetric graphite-outlined butter-colored paddle wings each side at the flower. The striped rump needs to take a full THIRD of the mark. Make the blossom and bee ONE compact interlocking silhouette, like a tiny paper-cut emblem. Thoughtful negative spaces, broad organic curves, no fiddly outlines inside the flower, no fur. Bold solid dusty cornflower/periwinkle violet background edge-to-edge (#6262B7). Mark colors warm ivory #FFF0CF, sunflower ochre #F9B92F, blue-black #29273E. Graphic, cheerful, sophisticated, slightly mischievous; not a big-eyed kids' character or emoji. The entire mark has a generous clear margin on all sides and fills around 70 percent of the canvas. Only the icon, square, solid spot colors with clean edges. No icon container, type, numbers, pattern, leaves, stem, seed dots, texture, shadow, gradients, drop shadows, 3D, sparkles, check marks or banking props.
